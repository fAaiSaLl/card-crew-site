Going Alone in Euchre: How a Loner Works and When to Call One

Updated: 2026-11-21

Short answer: going alone (a loner) means the player who makes trump plays the hand without their partner, who lays their cards face down and sits out. If the lone player takes all 5 tricks, their team scores 4 points instead of 2. If they take 3 or 4 tricks, it's the usual 1 point. If they take fewer than 3, they are euchred and the other team scores 2 points.

How to go alone

  1. Say it when you make trump. You announce "alone" at the moment you order up the turned card, pick it up as dealer, or name trump in the second round. You can't decide later, after seeing how the first trick goes.
  2. Your partner sits out. They put their five cards face down and don't play for the whole hand. Each trick has only three cards.
  3. The lead. As usual, the player to the dealer's left leads the first trick. If that player is the partner who's sitting out, the next player to the left leads instead.
  4. Play the five tricks. You need at least 3 to avoid being euchred, and all 5 to collect the 4-point bonus.

The loner scoring table

0, 1 or 2 (euchred)2The other team

Compare that with playing the same hand with your partner: all 5 tricks would score 2, and 3 or 4 tricks would score 1. So a loner only changes anything when you take every trick. It doubles the reward for a march, and in exchange you give up your partner's help.

Worked example

Your team has 6 points, the other team has 8. You're holding the right bower, the left bower, the ace of trump and two off-suit aces, so you order up and say "alone".

  • You take all 5 tricks: 6 + 4 = 10. You win the game, 10 to 8.
  • Had you played with your partner and taken all 5: 6 + 2 = 8. The game would go on, 8 to 8.
  • One of your aces gets trumped and you take 4 tricks: 6 + 1 = 7, they still lead 8 to 7. Same result as playing with your partner.
  • Things go badly and you take only 2 tricks: they score 2, 8 + 2 = 10, and they win the game.

That last line is the real risk. Without a partner, a hand that would normally be safe can get euchred if an opponent holds a trump you didn't expect.

When is a loner worth it?

  • You hold the top trumps. The right and left bower plus one or two more trumps is the classic loner hand. With them you can pull the opponents' trumps and then cash your off-suit aces.
  • Your off-suit cards are aces, or you're void. A king in a side suit can lose to an ace; a void lets you trump in.
  • You'd probably march anyway. If your partner can't add much, sitting them out costs little and doubles the points.
  • Think about the score. At 6 or more, a 4-point loner can end the game. When the other team is at 8 or 9, a euchre (2 points) hands them the win, so only go alone with a very safe hand.

If you're not sure you'll take all five, play with your partner. A loner that takes 3 or 4 tricks scores exactly the same 1 point as a normal hand, but with more risk of being euchred.

Table rules that vary

Euchre is played many different ways, so agree on these before the first deal:

  • Defending alone. Some tables let one defender also go alone against a loner. If that defender euchres the lone player, their team scores 4 instead of 2.
  • Ordering up your partner as dealer. In some groups, if you order your dealer-partner to pick up the card, you must go alone. Most tables don't use this rule.
  • Partner calls the loner. A few tables allow the maker's partner to be the one who goes alone, sitting the maker out instead.

Common questions

Does a loner score more if it takes 3 or 4 tricks?

No. In standard scoring, 3 or 4 tricks is 1 point whether you go alone or not. Only a march is worth more (4 instead of 2).

Do the defenders get extra points for euchring a loner?

Not in standard scoring: a euchre is worth 2 points either way. Tables that allow a defender to go alone give 4 for a lone euchre.

Can the dealer go alone?

Yes. The dealer can pick up the turned card and go alone, or name trump in the second round and go alone, like any other player.

Can I change my mind after saying "alone"?

No. Once you've announced it, your partner's cards go face down for the whole hand.
