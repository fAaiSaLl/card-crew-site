Left Bower and Right Bower in Euchre: Which Jacks Are Trump?

Updated: 2026-11-13

Short answer: in Euchre the right bower is the jack of the trump suit and it is the highest card in the game. The left bower is the other jack of the same colour, and it is the second-highest card. For that hand, the left bower stops belonging to its printed suit and counts as a trump in every way, including when you have to follow suit.

Which jack is which?

Hearts and diamonds are the red suits; spades and clubs are the black suits. Once trump is named, look at its colour:

TrumpRight bowerLeft bower
Hearts ♥J♥J♦
Diamonds ♦J♦J♥
Spades ♠J♠J♣
Clubs ♣J♣J♠

The name comes from the German word Bauer (farmer), an old name for the jack. "Right" and "left" don't refer to any position at the table; they are just the names of the two top cards.

How trump cards rank

Euchre is played with 24 cards: 9, 10, J, Q, K and A in four suits. The trump suit gets the two bowers, so it has 7 cards, ranked from highest to lowest:

  1. Right bower (jack of trump)
  2. Left bower (jack of the same colour)
  3. Ace of trump
  4. King of trump
  5. Queen of trump
  6. 10 of trump
  7. 9 of trump

The other suit of the same colour loses its jack, so it has only 5 cards that hand (A, K, Q, 10, 9). The two suits of the other colour keep all 6 cards, ranked A, K, Q, J, 10, 9.

The rule that causes arguments: following suit

Because the left bower is a trump, it is not part of its printed suit for that hand. With hearts as trump:

  • If a diamond is led and your only diamond-looking card is the J♦, you are actually out of diamonds. You can trump in with it, or throw something else.
  • If a diamond is led and you hold the J♦ and the A♦, you must play the A♦. The J♦ does not count as a diamond.
  • If a heart (trump) is led and your only trump is the J♦, you must play the J♦, because it is a heart this hand.

Playing the left bower as if it were still its printed suit, or holding it back when trump is led and it's your only trump, is a renege. Most tables penalise a renege by giving the other team 2 points, but penalties vary, so agree on it before you start.

Worked example

Your team leads 8 to 7. The turned-up card is the 10♥ and your partner orders it up, so hearts are trump and your team are the makers. You hold J♦, A♦, K♥, 9♣ and Q♠.

  1. An opponent leads the K♦. You must follow with the A♦ (your J♦ is a heart now). Your ace wins the trick.
  2. Later the 9♥ is led. Your trumps are the J♦ and K♥, so you can play either. You play the J♦: only the J♥ beats it, and it was played earlier in the hand, so you win.
  3. Your team ends the hand with 4 tricks out of 5.

Scoring: 3 or 4 tricks for the makers is 1 point, so the score becomes 8 + 1 = 9 to 7. If your team had taken only 2 tricks, you would have been euchred and the other team would score 2: 7 + 2 = 9, so they would lead 9 to 8.

Common questions

Is the left bower higher than the ace of trump?

Yes. Only the right bower beats it. The ace of trump is the third-best card.

Can the left bower be led as its own suit?

No. If you lead the J♦ when hearts are trump, you have led trump, and everyone must follow with hearts (or the J♦'s partner, the J♥) if they can.

What is the "best bower"?

Some groups add a Joker to the deck, called the best bower, which ranks above the right bower. It's a house rule, not standard Euchre, so check before you play.

What happens to the bowers if there is no trump?

Every hand that is played has a trump suit. If nobody orders up or names trump in either round, the cards are usually thrown in and the next player deals (some tables play "stick the dealer", where the dealer must name trump).
