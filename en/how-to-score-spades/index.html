How to Score Spades: Bids, Bags, Nil and the 500-Point Game

Updated: 2026-11-12

Short answer: in Spades each team scores 10 points for every trick it bid if it takes at least that many tricks, plus 1 point for each extra trick (a "bag"). If it takes fewer tricks than it bid, it loses 10 points per trick bid. The first team to 500 wins.

1. The bid (the contract)

After the deal, each of the four players says how many tricks they expect to win. Partners add their bids together: that total is the team's contract for the hand. With 13 tricks in a hand, the two contracts usually add up to around 11 to 13.

2. Making or missing the contract

  • Made: tricks taken ≥ contract → 10 × contract, plus 1 point per overtrick.
  • Set (missed): tricks taken < contract → −10 × contract. Overtricks don't matter when you're set.

3. Bags and the 10-bag penalty

Every trick above your contract is a bag. Bags give 1 point each, but they pile up across hands: every time a team collects 10 bags, it loses 100 points, and the count starts again from what's left over. That's why good players avoid taking tricks they don't need.

4. Nil and blind nil

  • Nil: a player bids zero tricks. If they take none: +100. If they take any: −100.
  • Blind nil: bid nil before looking at your cards: +200 / −200.
  • The nil player's tricks don't count toward the partner's bid. They count as bags for the team.

Common questions

What's a "Boston"?

Taking all 13 tricks in one hand. Many tables play it as a special bonus or an instant win; standard scoring doesn't change for it, so agree before you play.

Do bags reset after the penalty?

Yes. If you have 12 bags you lose 100 points and keep 2.

Can you go negative?

Yes. A team's score can drop below zero after a set or a failed nil.
