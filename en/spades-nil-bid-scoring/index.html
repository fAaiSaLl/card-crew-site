Nil in Spades: How Bidding Zero Is Scored, and When to Try It

Updated: 2026-11-18

Short answer: bidding nil in Spades means you promise to take zero tricks. If you take none, your team scores +100. If you take even one, your team loses 100. Your partner's bid is scored separately as normal, and any tricks you take while failing nil don't help your partner; they count as bags for your team. A blind nil, bid before looking at your cards, is worth +200 or −200.

How a nil is scored, step by step

When one player on a team bids nil, the team really has two separate contracts in the same hand:

  1. The nil: +100 if the nil player took 0 tricks, −100 if they took 1 or more.
  2. The partner's bid: scored on the partner's tricks only. Made: 10 points per trick bid plus 1 per extra trick (a bag). Missed: −10 per trick bid.
  3. Bags: the partner's extra tricks are bags, and so is every trick the nil player took. Each time a team reaches 10 bags, it loses 100 points.

Add the parts together and that's the team's score for the hand. A team can make one part and fail the other: the nil can succeed while the partner gets set, or the other way round.

Worked example

Before the hand, your team has 230 points and 7 bags. The other team has 310 points and 2 bags. You bid nil and your partner bids 5. The opponents bid 4 and 3, so their contract is 7.

If your nil works

You take 0 tricks. Your partner takes 6, and the opponents take the other 7.

  • Nil made: +100
  • Partner bid 5, took 6: 5 × 10 + 1 = +51, and 1 bag (7 + 1 = 8 bags, no penalty yet)
  • Your team this hand: 100 + 51 = +151, new score 230 + 151 = 381
  • Opponents bid 7, took 7: +70, new score 310 + 70 = 380

If your nil fails

Now say you get stuck with 3 tricks, your partner takes exactly 5, and the opponents take only 5.

  • Nil failed: −100
  • Partner bid 5, took 5: +50
  • Your 3 tricks become bags: 7 + 3 = 10 bags, so the bag penalty hits: −100, and your bag count goes back to 0
  • Your team this hand: −100 + 50 − 100 = −150, new score 230 − 150 = 80
  • Opponents bid 7, took 5: set, −70, new score 310 − 70 = 240

The same hand is a 301-point swing for your team depending on whether you duck every trick. That's why nil is exciting, and why it shouldn't be bid lightly.

When is nil worth bidding?

  • Few or low spades. Spades are trump, so a hand with the ace or king of spades, or four or more spades, is risky: you may be forced to win a trick.
  • Low cards in every suit. 2s, 3s and 4s are your friends. A lone ace or king in a side suit is the classic way a nil dies.
  • Short suits help. If you run out of a suit early, you can throw away dangerous high cards when that suit is led.
  • A strong partner. Your partner's job is to "cover" you: play high on suits you're weak in so you can play low under them. A partner with a lot of high cards makes your nil much safer.
  • The score matters. Behind late in the game, a nil can catch up quickly. Ahead and near 500, a failed nil can hand the game away.

House rules to agree on first

Nil is one of the most varied parts of Spades, so settle these before the first deal:

  • Value: most games use 100 for nil and 200 for blind nil, but some use 50.
  • Blind nil limits: many tables only allow blind nil when a team is behind by 100 points or more, and some let the partners swap one or two cards afterwards.
  • Failed-nil tricks: in standard scoring (and in Card Crew) they count as bags but don't help the partner make their bid. Some tables let them count toward the partner's bid instead.
  • Both partners nil: allowed in most games, each nil scored on its own. Some tables ban it.

Common questions

Does a nil count as a bid of zero for the team's contract?

No. The team's contract is just the partner's bid. The nil is a separate +100/−100 bonus on top.

If my nil fails, do my tricks give points?

No points, only bags. In the example above, your 3 tricks scored nothing but pushed your team to 10 bags and the −100 penalty.

Can the opponents try to break my nil?

Yes, and they usually will. Leading low cards in a suit where they think you're stuck with a high one is their best way to force a trick on you.

What if both teams bid nil?

Each nil is scored for its own team. It doesn't change anything else.
