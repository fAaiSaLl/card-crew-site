How to Keep Score in Euchre with a 4 and a 6

Updated: 2026-11-24

Short answer: each team takes a 4 and a 6 from the cards Euchre doesn't use, one team in red and the other in black. You lay one card face down over the other so that the number of pips you can see is your score. Since 4 + 6 = 10, the two cards cover every score from 0 up to 10, which is exactly what you need for a game to 10.

Why a 4 and a 6?

Euchre is played with only 24 cards (9, 10, jack, queen, king and ace of each suit). That leaves the 2s through 8s of a standard deck sitting in the box, so most tables use them as score markers instead of looking for a pen. The 4 and the 6 are the usual pair because together they show 10 pips, the winning score.

The rule to remember

A card lying face up counts its pips. A card lying face down counts nothing, and it hides whatever pips it covers. So you just slide the face-down card until the right number of pips is visible. Only count the big pips in the middle of the card, not the small symbols in the corners.

Card positions for every score

ScoreHow to lay the cards
0Both cards face down (or the 6 face down covering the whole 4)
14 face up, 6 face down on top showing 1 pip of the 4
24 face up, 6 face down on top showing 2 pips
34 face up, 6 face down on top showing 3 pips
44 face up with all 4 pips showing, 6 face down underneath
56 face up, 4 face down on top hiding 1 pip
66 face up with all 6 pips showing, 4 face down underneath
76 face up, 4 face up on top showing 1 of its pips (6 + 1)
86 face up, 4 face up showing 2 of its pips (6 + 2)
96 face up, 4 face up showing 3 of its pips (6 + 3)
10Both cards face up side by side: you've won

For 7 to 9, both cards are face up: all 6 pips of the 6 stay visible, and the 4 is tucked partly under it so only 1, 2 or 3 of its pips peek out. Plenty of tables do it slightly differently, for example turning the 6 sideways across the 4. The method doesn't matter as long as everyone agrees how to read it before the first hand.

How many points to add

After each hand, the team that scored moves its cards to the new total. In standard Euchre scoring:

  • Makers take 3 or 4 tricks: +1
  • Makers take all 5 tricks (a march): +2
  • A player going alone takes all 5 tricks: +4
  • Makers take fewer than 3 tricks (euchred): the other team gets +2

Worked example

Here's a short game, with the cards each team shows after every hand.

HandWhat happenedUsThem
1We make trump, take 3 tricks: +11 (4 up, 1 pip showing)0
2We get euchred: they score +212 (4 up, 2 pips showing)
3We march: 1 + 2 = 33 (4 up, 3 pips showing)2
4Our partner goes alone and takes all 5: 3 + 4 = 77 (6 up, 1 pip of the 4)2
5They make trump, take 4 tricks: 2 + 1 = 373
6They euchre us: 3 + 2 = 575 (6 up, 1 pip hidden)
7We take 3 tricks: 7 + 1 = 88 (6 up, 2 pips of the 4)5
8We march: 8 + 2 = 1010: we win5

Final score: 10 to 5. Notice hand 4: the loner jumped the score from 3 straight to 7, so the cards went from "4 face up" to "6 face up with the 4 on top" in one move.

Common questions

Can I use other cards?

Yes. Some people use two 5s (5 + 5 = 10), which reads the same way. Any pair works if the table agrees.

What if a team goes past 10?

The game ends as soon as a team reaches 10 or more, so a final score of 11 or 12 is normal. You don't need cards for it; just say the total.

Why do teams use different colours?

So nobody mixes up whose cards are whose. One team uses the red 4 and 6, the other the black ones, and each team keeps its pair in front of one player.

What's the most common mistake?

Bumping the cards while playing. A face-down card that slides by half an inch changes the score, so keep the markers away from where tricks are gathered, and say the score out loud after each hand.
